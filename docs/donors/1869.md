# 1869

## Evidence inspected

- [1869 DOS manual](https://oldgamesdownload.com/wp-content/uploads/1869_Manual_DOS_EN.pdf) documents port offices, cargo offered/required by dealers, full-load guidance, destination route planning, navigation waypoints, weather/current choices, ship availability and steamship fuel range.
- [1869 manual transcription](https://www.abandonwaredos.com/docawd.php?idg=958&sf=1869manual.txt&sg=1869&st=manual) was used to cross-check cargo purchasing, warehouse/port transfer, passenger destination behavior and route planning.
- [1869 overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1869_%28video_game%29) confirms the core loop of purchasing goods at one port, moving them and selling them at another, with passenger transport as a related mechanic.

## Selected mechanism

The reusable kernel is condition-aware route planning. A carrier chooses an accessible route through optional navigation points, using effective passage time rather than blindly taking the first graph path. A maximum passage budget can reject a course for a limited-range vessel. The selected course is then carried into the same physical Shipment route used for cargo custody and settlement.

## Rejected

This pass does not add ship auctions, company offices, taxes, naval blockades, political port closures, crew hiring, passenger cabins, ship construction, or the historical 1869 trade catalogue. Those are either later finance/passenger work or scenario content outside this transport kernel.

## Canonical translation

`best_route` reads `Route`, `RouteCondition`, terrain and transport mode, and chooses the least effective-time accessible path. `dispatch_freight(route_ids=...)` validates and stores that exact physical path on the canonical Shipment. It does not advance time or move cargo during route selection.

## Tests and uncertainty

Tests cover waypoint selection under adverse route conditions, maximum travel budgets, explicit physical shipment routes and rejection of inaccessible courses. The manuals provide reliable behavioral evidence, but exact original wind/current scoring and steamship range formulas remain intentionally unspecified.
